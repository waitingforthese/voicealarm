# LifeAlarm – Navagraha Mantra Authenticity & Pronunciation Reference V1

## Selection policy
- The app uses a short daily-japa set for the Planetary Tara Aaradhana.
- These are moola/samanya-style forms rather than long Vedic or tantric prayoga texts.
- We do NOT label these as the only authentic forms. Sanskrit traditions contain multiple valid mantra paddhatis and textual variants.
- Longer Vedic/tantric prayoga texts remain reference material and are not silently substituted into the daily TTS sequence.

## Selected daily-japa texts
1. Surya: ॐ घृणि सूर्याय नमः।
2. Chandra: ॐ सोम सोमाय नमः।
3. Mangala: ॐ अं अंगारकाय नमः।
4. Budha: ॐ बुं बुधाय नमः।
5. Guru: ॐ बृं बृहस्पतये नमः।
6. Shukra: ॐ शुं शुक्राय नमः।
7. Shani: ॐ शं शनैश्चराय नमः।
8. Rahu: ॐ रां राहवे नमः।
9. Ketu: ॐ कें केतवे नमः।

## Reference hierarchy
- SanskritDocuments: Navagraha Mantrah, Navagraha Japamantra, Puranokta/Tantrokta/Vedokta prayoga collections. These demonstrate that multiple textual traditions exist and provide source attributions for longer mantra forms.
- Drik Panchang: individual Navagraha mantra pages, including moola/samanya, bija, Gayatri and pranama forms. The selected short forms for most planets are cross-checked there.
- Brihaspati: the short form ॐ बृं बृहस्पतये नमः is retained as the practical daily-japa form and cross-checked against traditional Brihaspati mantra references.

## Pronunciation policy
- Sanskrit Devanagari is retained as the canonical display text.
- TTS uses the same canonical Devanagari text, not an English transliteration.
- The app's speech-rate control may slow the utterance but must not rewrite the Sanskrit text.
- Third-party recorded audio is NOT embedded unless a lawful/licensed recording is supplied or obtained.

## Important distinction
A mantra can be textually traditional while its audio realization varies by regional/recitation tradition. For Vedic mantra with svara, a trained traditional reciter and a licensed recording are preferred over generic TTS. The short daily-japa forms in this feature are not presented as Vedic-svara recitations.
